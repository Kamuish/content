# Impact of BERV on the tellurics

The position of the telluric features is constant through time, as the motion of the atmosphere occurs in the referential of the spectrograph, but their depth is not, as it will depend on the weather conditions and position of our target on the sky. Changes in the concentration of different molecules in the atmosphere will, obviously, be reflected on the depth of associated features. On top of that, the lower the star is on the sky, the larger the optical path of the light and the deeper the telluric features. Even though the position is fixed on the referential of the spectrograph, we must recall that we change our frame of reference with the barycentric correction. This means that after this correction, the telluric lines will appear Doppler-shifted by the  [[20240917T10142_BERV|BERV]] value associated with the observation. In other words, if we observe the same star during one year, the tellurics will fall in a window of $\pm$ 30 km/s (the yearly BERV variation). This effect can be seen in Figure [[#^1f089e]] where the BERV correction shifts the location of the telluric features (top pannel), whilst the observations on the spectrograph referential (bottom panel) are aligned.
