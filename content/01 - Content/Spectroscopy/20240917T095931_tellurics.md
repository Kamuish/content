# Tellurics

High-resolution [[2024_Spectrographs|Spectrographs]] are ground-based instruments, consequence of the large complexity and high monetary investments needed for a space-based observatory. When observing from Earth we find, as one would expect, that the atmosphere's signature will be imprinted in the spectra, as it absorbs the stellar radiation and creates absorption features, also known as telluric lines.  From *Cunha et al. 2014* and *Figueira et al. 2012*   we know that this effect strongly depends on wavelength, airmass and weather conditions during the observations. More worryingly is the fact that the shallower features, also known as *micro-telluric* can impact our Radial Velocities at the 10 cm/s level, which is on par with the signal introduced by a Earth-like planet orbiting a Sun-like star.

When handing the telluric features we have to both account for the [[20240917T095949_Impact of BERV on the tellurics|Impact of BERV on the tellurics]] but also the effect of  [[20240917T10015_Telluric contamination on the stellar spectra|Telluric contamination on the stellar spectra]]. To account for this source of contamination on our data we can either reject the wavelength regions that are affected by them, or attempt to correct them through models or data-driven approaches:

1) Removal of telluric features
2) Correction of telluric features
3) Data-driven methods to disentangle telluric features

Despite the large number of available approaches we still face large issues. First, the available tools for the correction of telluric features often fail to provide a meaningful uncertain propagation to their estimates for the telluric correction. Second, the rejection of spectral regions can also lead to a large loss of RV content for a single observation at optical wavelengths, i.e. without considering the effect that the barycentric motion ([[20240917T10142_BERV|BERV]])  will have in the position of those features throughout the year. Furthermore, this is only viable at optical wavelengths, as the cost of rejecting tellurics based on transmittance would be unbearable in the near infra-red regions.
